# Terminology

**Acceptance criterion:** A source-bound condition that must pass for a deliverable to be complete.

**Action constraint:** A machine-checkable or reviewable rule applied to the exact next action, not merely recorded for later.

**Baseline:** The before-state used to distinguish existing conditions from change-induced effects.

**Blocker:** A subordinate obstacle with a minimum unblock condition and return step, not a replacement objective.

**Consumer oracle:** Evidence tied to the result observed by the intended user, downstream system, or external resource.

**Evidence limit:** The strongest claim a given evidence route can support. Also called a proof ceiling in the normative reference.

**Interaction closure:** Coverage of reachable scenario combinations that can change harm, state, oracle, recovery, or consumer result.

**Objective contract:** The source-bound map of requested outcomes, acceptance criteria, constraints, means, and assumptions.

**Objective-necessity link:** A statement of why a material action is on the critical path and what source-bound claim would fail without it.

**Outcome link:** Public shorthand for objective-necessity link.

**Semantic authority boundary:** The rule that legacy behavior, examples, tests, or prior notes are authoritative only for the externally relevant semantics they actually define.

**Semantic recomposition:** The act of proving that local and relational evidence together still entails the original system-level claim.

**Coordinator and Executor:** Public shorthand for the Supervisor and Worker topology.

**Learning ledger:** Public shorthand for a project or global knowledge master.

**Supervisor and Worker:** A precise topology where one thread preserves objective integrity and consumes Worker results, while one primary Worker performs the objective-bound work. The public docs also call this Coordinator and Executor when introducing the idea.
